## What could be informative beyond familiar cap effects?

The proposed mechanism is **uneven sensitivity to the source boundary**. A cap removes some source TPs; to attain the same source recall the selector may admit lower-scoring candidates on other source images. The resulting boundary interval may contain many more night TPs per eligible object than COCO-reference TPs. Target images can therefore respond despite little direct target truncation. For genuinely nested score-prefix cap outputs with the same matching order, feasible `t_q <= t_p`, hence `B >= 0` and `D <= 0`. Opposite signs require explaining the actual native policy/tie/matching difference before claiming a mechanism. An apparent recall benefit of the tighter cap can be an operating-point tradeoff rather than better detection. This is a refutable explanation of where an operating difference originates, not a new discovery that caps remove boxes.

Two quantities must not be conflated: source-image influence on the selected boundary, and target TP/FP mass between the two boundaries. A large numerical score gap is not itself evidence of either; strictly monotone score transformations can stretch it without changing detections. Count actual TP/FP changes and retain the thresholds at their recorded precision. Counting that interval's TP mass merely explains `B` arithmetically. A defensible empirical contribution would need reproducible source influence, disproportionate target response and a useful decision consequence beyond ordinary finite-source instability.

The [Nov 9 audit](2026-11-09_novelty_audit.md) and [review resolution](../reviews/2026-11-09_gpt_research_program_resolution.md) already establish the relevant limits. Dave et al. (P10) covers cross-category budget coupling; Hosang et al. (P8/P9) covers proposal budgets and suppression; Kuzucu et al. (P2) covers threshold-dependent calibration and shifted evaluation. TIDE (P11) prevents treating an error identity as a new taxonomy. These are inherited audited readings, not new Astra replications. No additional primary-source check was needed; Lee's inaccessible full method and AegisBench's identity discrepancies remain unresolved, not novelty exclusions.

The strongest alternatives and controls are small enough to use the existing outputs:

| Alternative explanation | Necessary comparison and consequence |

Native violations require a named pipeline explanation, not a new theory. |
